How the market feeds the model

For months that are over, the fund return is skill + beta × stock-market return + the rest in cash. The stock-market return is the real S&P 500 month-end to month-end move. The cash rate is the real 3-month T-bill. For months still to come, the model uses the plan return unless a scenario changes it.

D. E. Shaw's latest public 13F filings SEC EDGAR

A 13F lists only the US-listed stocks and options a large manager held at quarter-end, filed up to 45 days later. It leaves out non-US securities, bonds, most derivatives and shorts, so it is a partial, delayed lens on the firm. This page is not affiliated with D. E. Shaw.

The five ideas, in plain words

Budget
The plan made before the year: how much money we will manage, how much we will earn, how much we will spend.
Forecast
Our best guess for the full year after real months have happened. Real months go in, the rest of the year is re-estimated.
Scenario
A "what if?" run: what if markets rise, what if they fall and investors take money out.
Variance
The gap between plan and reality, plus the reason. This tool splits the gap into market returns, money in and out, and hiring and cost rates.
High-water mark
The fund's highest price so far. Performance fees are only charged on gains above it, so a fund that falls must first win back its losses before it earns performance fees again.
Beta and alpha
Beta is how much of the stock market's move the fund shares. Alpha is the extra the manager adds on top. Both are assumptions here.

Where the numbers come from

  • FRED (St. Louis Fed): S&P 500 daily close, VIX, 3-month T-bill, 10-year Treasury. End-of-day data with a short delay.
  • Finnhub (optional): latest SPY quote, if a key is set.
  • SEC EDGAR: public 13F filings for the 13F tab.
  • Everything about the firm itself (fees, headcount, costs, flows) is sample data.
